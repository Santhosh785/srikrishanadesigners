Your wedding outfits are usually the most expensive garments you will ever buy, and they are worn on days when nothing can go wrong. We plan bridal orders around your function dates, not around our convenience, and we build trial fittings into the schedule so that the final outfit fits on the day, not just on paper.

## What do we make for brides?

- **Bridal blouses** for the muhurtham saree, usually silk with hand aari or maggam work on the sleeves and back.
- **Lehengas** stitched from fabric you buy, or semi-stitched lehengas that need fitting and finishing.
- **Half-saree (langa voni) sets** for pre-wedding ceremonies.
- **Reception gowns and Indo-western outfits**, including lehenga-style skirts with crop tops and jacket sets.
- **Outfits for the family**, so that the bride's mother and sisters can be planned along with her.

## How much does bridal stitching cost?

Stitching a bridal blouse with heavy hand work costs significantly more than a plain designer blouse, because a single bridal back can take several days of embroidery. A lehenga set costs more again, as the skirt needs panels (kalis), lining, can-can and a finished waistband, and the dupatta needs borders.

We give a written quote for each outfit once we have seen the fabric and design. The price of fabric, ready embroidered borders or stones you choose to buy is separate.

## How long does bridal stitching take?

Here is the timeline we recommend for a wedding with hand-embroidered outfits:

| Weeks before the wedding | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 8 weeks | First meeting: designs, fabric check, measurements, quote |
| 6–7 weeks | Embroidery begins on the framed fabric |
| 4 weeks | First trial fitting after the basic cut |
| 2 weeks | Second trial fitting with embroidery and assembly done |
| 1 week | Final fit check and delivery |

If you start later than this, we can still help, but you will have fewer choices in embroidery and fewer fittings.

## What is the process?

1. **Plan the wardrobe.** List every function and which outfit goes with each. This tells us which outfit must be ready first.
2. **Check fabric.** We confirm there is enough fabric for the design, including sleeves and dupatta borders.
3. **Measure and quote.** We take full measurements and give a written price and delivery date for each piece.
4. **Embroider.** Hand work is done on a frame before the garment is assembled.
5. **Trial fittings.** You try each outfit at two stages, and we correct the fit.
6. **Final finishing.** Hooks, zips, latkans, hemming, pressing and a last check before pickup.

## What should I bring to my first bridal meeting?

- All the fabric: saree or lehenga pieces, blouse piece and dupatta.
- Reference photos for each outfit, including the back of the blouse.
- The innerwear and footwear you plan to wear, since heel height changes lehenga length.
- Your list of function dates.

## Tips from our bridal fittings

- **Fix the heel height first.** We hem the lehenga for the footwear you will actually wear.
- **Move in the trial fitting.** Sit down, raise your arms and bend slightly. A bridal blouse needs to survive a long ceremony, not just photographs.
- **Keep the reception outfit simpler.** You will be standing and greeting guests for hours, so comfort matters. Our post on [lehenga vs saree for a reception](/blog/lehenga-vs-saree-for-reception/) compares the options.
