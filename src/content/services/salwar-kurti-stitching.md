Everyday clothes are the ones you wear most, so their fit matters every single day. A kurti that pulls at the armhole or a churidar that bags at the knee gets pushed to the back of the cupboard. We stitch salwar suits, kurtis, churidars and anarkalis to your measurements, and we alter ready-made pieces so they finally fit the way they should.

## What can we stitch?

- **Kurtis** in straight, A-line, side-slit, high-low and front-open styles.
- **Salwars** including Patiala, straight, semi-Patiala and dhoti styles.
- **Churidars and pants**, from tight-fitting churidars to straight cigarette pants and palazzos.
- **Anarkalis** in short and floor-length versions, with as many kalis as the fabric allows.
- **Alterations** on ready-made kurtis, suits and dresses.

## How much does salwar and kurti stitching cost?

A simple unlined kurti is the most affordable item we stitch. The price goes up with lining, piping, patchwork on the neck, extra panels and floor length. An anarkali takes the most time because of the number of panels and the long hem. Alterations are priced by the job, for example shortening a hem costs less than reshaping the shoulders.

You will get the price before we cut, and it does not change unless you change the design.

## How long does it take?

Most kurtis and salwar sets are ready in 4–7 days. Anarkalis and lined festive suits may take a week or more, especially before festivals like Diwali, when everyone wants new clothes at the same time. Alterations usually take 2–3 days.

## What is the process?

1. **Show us the fabric and the style.** A photo of the neck and sleeve you want is enough.
2. **Measure or copy.** We take your measurements, or copy a kurti or salwar that already fits you well.
3. **Confirm details.** Length, slit height, neck depth, sleeve length, pockets and lining are decided now.
4. **Stitch.** We cut and stitch, with piping and finishing as agreed.
5. **Pickup and try-on.** Try it on at the studio so any small correction can be made immediately.

## What should I bring?

- All the fabric pieces, including the dupatta if it needs hemming.
- A kurti, salwar or churidar that fits you well.
- The footwear you usually wear with the outfit, since it changes the right length for churidars and anarkalis.

## What alterations can we do on ready-made outfits?

Ready-made kurtis are cut for a standard size, so they often fit at the bust but not at the waist, or fit in length but not at the shoulders. We can take in side seams, shorten the length and sleeves, raise the shoulder, deepen or reshape the neck, add lining to a sheer kurti, and shorten or narrow pants and churidars. Letting a garment out depends on the seam allowance inside, so we check that before promising.

## Tips for everyday outfits that fit

- **Ask for side pockets.** They can be added to most kurtis and many people love them once they have them.
- **Pre-wash cotton fabric.** Cotton can shrink in the first wash. Soak it in water and dry it before you bring it in.
- **Choose the bottom for your body and routine.** Our [salwar vs churidar fitting guide](/blog/salwar-vs-churidar-fitting-guide/) explains which works for which occasion.
