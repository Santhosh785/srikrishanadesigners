A blouse decides how a saree sits on you. The same silk saree can look elegant or awkward depending on how well the blouse fits at the shoulder, bust and back. At our studio we cut every blouse individually, from your measurements or from a blouse you already love, and we finish it with the lining, closures and detailing that the design needs.

## What kinds of blouses do we stitch?

We stitch everything from everyday cotton blouses to heavy bridal pieces:

- **Simple lined blouses** for daily wear and office sarees.
- **Princess-cut and katori blouses**, which use extra seams for a closer, more shaped fit through the bust.
- **Padded blouses** with sewn-in cups, useful for deep necks, sheer fabrics and backless designs.
- **Designer necks** such as boat neck, high neck, collar, sweetheart, keyhole and deep back with dori.
- **Embroidered blouses** with hand aari or maggam work on the sleeves, neckline and back.
- **Finishing details** like latkans, tassels, piping, contrast borders and potli buttons.

## How much does designer blouse stitching cost?

The price depends on three things: the cut, the inner structure and the hand work. A plain lined blouse is our starting point. A princess cut or padded blouse costs more because it takes extra pattern work and fitting time. Embroidery is priced separately, because a small motif on the sleeve and a fully worked bridal back are very different jobs.

We quote aari and maggam work only after seeing the design reference and your fabric. That way the price you agree to is the price you pay, without surprises at delivery.

## How long does it take?

A simple blouse is usually ready in 5–7 days. Designer cuts with padding may need an extra day or two for the trial fitting. Heavy aari work takes 2–3 weeks, and longer during the wedding season. If you have a function date, tell us when you order so we can plan backwards from it.

## What is the process?

1. **Discuss the design.** Bring photos or screenshots of what you like. We will tell you honestly whether it suits the fabric and your comfort level.
2. **Take measurements.** We measure you in person or copy a well-fitting sample blouse.
3. **Agree on price and date.** You get a clear quote, and we take an advance.
4. **Cut and embroider.** If the design has hand work, the fabric is framed and embroidered before stitching.
5. **Trial fitting.** For fitted, padded and bridal blouses, you try the blouse before final finishing.
6. **Finish and deliver.** We complete the hemming, hooks or zip, latkans and pressing.

## What should I bring to my first fitting?

- The blouse piece, and the saree if possible, so we can match lining colour and border placement.
- Your best-fitting blouse, even if it is old. It tells us a lot about the fit you are used to.
- The innerwear you will wear on the day. Bust measurements change with a different bra, and padded designs depend on it.
- Reference photos of the neck, sleeve and back you want.

## Practical tips for a better blouse

- **Decide sleeve length early.** Elbow and full sleeves need more fabric than the blouse piece sometimes provides.
- **Do not lose weight for the fitting.** If you are planning a diet before a wedding, tell us. We can leave extra seam allowance so the blouse can be taken in later.
- **Choose lining carefully.** Cotton lining is cooler and more comfortable for long functions; satin lining slides on more easily but can feel warm.
- **Keep deep backs practical.** A deep back with dori looks beautiful but needs the right innerwear. Ask us before finalising.
