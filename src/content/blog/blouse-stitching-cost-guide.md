"How much for a blouse?" is the first question almost every customer asks us, and the honest answer is: it depends on which blouse. A plain cotton blouse for office wear and a bridal blouse with a fully embroidered back are both called "blouses", but one takes a few hours and the other can take days of hand work. This guide breaks down what you actually pay for, so you can compare quotes fairly.

## How much does blouse stitching cost in 2026?

These are the typical ranges we see for stitching charges only. Fabric is not included, and prices vary between cities and studios.

| Blouse type | Typical stitching price | What makes it cost more |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple unlined blouse | ₹400–₹600 | Extra darts, contrast piping |
| Simple lined blouse | ₹500–₹800 | Fancy lining, side zip |
| Designer neck (boat, collar, high neck) | ₹800–₹1,500 | Deep back with dori, keyhole details |
| Princess cut or katori blouse | ₹1,000–₹2,000 | More panels and a trial fitting |
| Padded blouse | ₹1,200–₹2,500 | Shaped cups, backless or sheer designs |
| Aari or maggam work blouse | ₹3,000 and above | Coverage, stones, zardosi, beadwork |

Treat these as a starting point for a conversation, not a fixed rate card. Your tailor should give you a specific quote after seeing your fabric and design.

## What actually drives the price?

### The cut

A basic blouse has a front, a back and two sleeves, with a few darts for shaping. A princess cut replaces the darts with curved seams running over the bust, which gives a smoother fit but needs more careful pattern work. Katori cuts add a separate cup panel. Every extra panel means extra cutting, stitching and fitting time.

### The inner structure

Lining, padding and closures are invisible from outside, but they decide how the blouse feels and how long it lasts:

- **Lining** stops the blouse fabric from stretching and makes it comfortable against the skin.
- **Padding** gives shape to deep necks and sheer fabrics, so you do not need separate padded innerwear.
- **Closures** such as front hooks, back hooks, side zips or back zips each take different amounts of work.

### Hand embroidery

This is where prices vary the most. Aari and maggam work are done by hand, using a hooked needle on fabric stretched in a frame. A small butta motif on each sleeve may take a few hours. A full bridal back with a peacock or temple design, filled with zari, stones and beads, can take several days. The price reflects those hours and the materials, so it is always quoted per design. Our post on [how long an aari work blouse takes](/blog/how-long-does-aari-work-blouse-take/) explains the timing in detail.

### Finishing details

Latkans, tassels, potli buttons, contrast piping, lace and cut-work borders are often priced as add-ons. They are small in size but can take real time, especially handmade latkans.

### Urgency

If you need a blouse in a day or two, it has to be moved ahead of other orders. Many studios charge an express fee for this.

## Why is a cheap blouse sometimes expensive in the end?

A low quote is not a bargain if the blouse has to be redone. The most common problems we see with blouses brought to us for correction are:

1. A tight armhole that restricts arm movement.
2. A neckline that gapes or slips off the shoulder.
3. Wrong bust point placement, which causes wrinkles across the front.
4. No seam allowance left, so the blouse cannot be loosened later.
5. Lining that is shorter or tighter than the outer fabric, causing puckering.

Fixing these can cost almost as much as stitching a new blouse, and sometimes the fabric cannot be saved at all. For a silk saree blouse piece that matches only one saree, that loss matters.

## How do you compare quotes from different tailors?

Ask every tailor the same questions:

- Is lining included, and what kind?
- Is there a trial fitting before finishing?
- Are padding, zip and latkans included or extra?
- What is the delivery date, and is there an express charge?
- Will you alter it free if the fit is wrong because of the stitching?
- How much advance do you take?

Once you have the same information from each, the comparison is fair. If you are booking for a wedding, our [checklist for choosing a bridal tailor](/blog/how-to-choose-a-bridal-tailor/) covers what else to look for.

## How can you keep blouse stitching costs down?

- **Bring a well-fitting sample blouse.** It saves fitting time and reduces the risk of corrections.
- **Choose embroidery placement wisely.** Work on the sleeves and back neckline gives most of the visual effect at a fraction of the cost of full coverage.
- **Order early.** You avoid express charges and get better attention during the wedding season.
- **Bundle orders.** If you have several blouses for a family function, order them together and ask about the total.
