Brides often ask whether they should choose aari or maggam work, as if they were two different crafts. In practice they are very close, and the decisions that actually affect your blouse are the design, the materials and the coverage. Here is what the terms mean and what to ask instead.

## What is the difference between aari and maggam work?

Both are hand embroidery done with a hooked needle while the fabric is stretched tight on a wooden frame (the maggam or adda). The hook pulls thread through in a fast chain stitch, and the same frame is used to attach zari, sequins, beads and stones.

- **Aari** is the name used most in Tamil Nadu and many other parts of India.
- **Maggam** is the name used most in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, where the frame itself is called a maggam.

Some people use "maggam" to mean heavier, more embellished bridal work and "aari" for finer thread work, but that is a habit, not a rule. Ask to see samples rather than relying on the name.

## What actually changes the look?

| Factor | Lighter look | Heavier look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threads | Silk or cotton thread only | Zari, metallic and zardosi |
| Embellishment | Few or no stones | Stones, beads, cutdana, mirror |
| Coverage | Sleeve hems and neck border | Full sleeves, back and front |
| Design density | Open motifs with fabric showing | Filled motifs, little fabric showing |
| Motifs | Small butis, creepers | Peacocks, mangoes, temple and figure work |

## How long does aari or maggam work take?

Time depends on coverage and density far more than the name. A simple sleeve border takes much less time than a fully covered bridal blouse with zardosi and stones. Our post on [how long an aari work blouse takes](/blog/how-long-does-aari-work-blouse-take/) goes through realistic timelines and why rushing shows in the finish.

As a rule, plan the heaviest bridal blouse first and leave time for two trial fittings around the embroidery.

## What decides the cost?

We quote after seeing the design, because two blouses with the "same" motif can differ a lot. The main cost factors are:

1. **Coverage area** — sleeves only, sleeves and back, or the whole blouse.
2. **Density** — how much of the fabric is filled.
3. **Materials** — zari quality, stones, beads and their number.
4. **Motif complexity** — figures and fine shading take longer than repeating creepers.
5. **Timeline** — urgent work usually costs more.

## Which is better for a bridal blouse?

For a Tamil muhurtham blouse on a Kanchipuram silk, many brides choose zari-rich work on the sleeves and back that echoes the saree border, with lighter work on the front. For a reception lehenga or a georgette saree, stone and bead work catches light well in the evening.

Match the work to the saree:

- Heavy silk with a wide border: keep motifs bold and related to the border.
- Light fabrics: avoid very heavy stones that pull the fabric.
- Busy sarees: choose lighter embroidery so the blouse does not compete.

## How can you judge the quality of hand embroidery?

Whatever it is called, good hand work shares the same signs. When you look at samples:

- **Even chain stitches** with consistent size, no loose loops.
- **Firmly attached stones and beads** that do not move when you run a finger over them.
- **Clean outlines** where motifs meet the fabric, without puckering.
- **A neat reverse side**, with no long loose threads that can catch.
- **Symmetry** between the two sleeves and on either side of the back.
- **Flat fabric** around the work. Puckering usually means the fabric was not stretched evenly on the frame.

Also ask whether the embroidery is done in-house or sent out, and how the cut pieces will be stored while the work is in progress.

## How should you care for an embroidered blouse?

- Dry clean only, and tell the cleaner about the stones and zari.
- Store flat or folded with soft cotton or muslin between folds, never in plastic for long.
- Keep away from perfume and deodorant, which can tarnish zari.
- Air it after wearing before putting it away.
- Check for loose stones after each wear and fix them early.
