A silk saree blouse, especially one with zari borders or hand aari work, is an investment of fabric, time and skill. With some simple habits it can stay beautiful for many years and many functions. Most of the damage we see in blouses brought in for repair is not from wear, but from the way they were washed and stored. This guide covers what to do after each wear, how to clean, and how to store.

## How should you care for a silk blouse after wearing it?

Do these three things before putting it away:

1. **Air it out.** Turn the blouse inside out and hang it on a padded hanger in shade for a few hours. This lets sweat and moisture evaporate.
2. **Check for stains.** Look at the underarms, the neckline and the front. Stains that are treated early come out more easily.
3. **Remove accessories.** Take off safety pins and brooches. Pins left in for weeks can rust and leave marks.

Never put a worn silk blouse straight into a cupboard, and never store it while it is even slightly damp.

## Should silk blouses be washed or dry cleaned?

| Blouse type | Recommended cleaning | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plain silk, no embroidery | Dry clean; careful cold hand wash only if the fabric is colourfast | Silk can bleed colour and shrink |
| Silk with zari border | Dry clean | Water and detergent tarnish zari |
| Aari or maggam work | Dry clean | Stones, beads and threads can loosen or stain |
| Bridal blouse | Dry clean, by someone experienced with bridal wear | Heavy work and expensive fabric |
| Cotton lining only stained | Spot clean the lining carefully | Avoids wetting the whole blouse |

If you choose to hand wash a plain silk blouse, test for colour bleeding first by dabbing a hidden seam with a wet white cloth. Use cold water and a mild liquid detergent meant for silk. Do not soak, scrub or wring. Roll it in a towel to remove water, and dry flat in shade.

## How do you deal with common stains?

- **Sweat marks at the underarms.** The most common problem. Air the blouse after each wear and use dress shields for long functions. Point the stain out to your dry cleaner.
- **Oil or food.** Blot, do not rub. Sprinkle a little talcum powder to absorb oil, then take it for dry cleaning soon.
- **Turmeric and kumkum.** These stain silk quickly. Blot gently and take it to a dry cleaner the same week.
- **Perfume and deodorant.** These can discolour silk and tarnish zari. Apply them before you dress and let them dry.

Avoid home remedies like lemon, vinegar or bleach on silk. They can permanently change the colour.

## How should you store silk blouses?

### Fold, do not hang long term

Hanging heavy embroidered blouses for months stretches the shoulders. For short periods between wears, a padded hanger is fine. For long storage, fold the blouse.

### Wrap in cotton or muslin

Wrap each blouse in a soft cotton cloth or muslin. An old, clean cotton saree or dhoti works well. Avoid plastic covers; they trap moisture and speed up zari tarnishing and fungus.

### Keep embroidery protected

Fold with the embroidered side inside, and place a layer of tissue or cotton cloth over the work so stones and beads do not catch on other fabric.

### Control moisture and insects

- Store in a dry cupboard, not against an outside wall that gets damp.
- Use neem leaves or cedar blocks wrapped in cloth to keep insects away.
- Do not put naphthalene balls in direct contact with silk.
- In humid months, open the cupboard and air the clothes once in a while.

### Refold every few months

Silk weakens along fixed folds, especially where there is zari. Unfold and refold along different lines every two to three months.

## How should you iron a silk blouse?

- Iron on the reverse side, on the silk setting or low heat.
- Place a thin cotton cloth between the iron and the blouse.
- Do not press directly on zari, stones or beads.
- Do not spray water on silk; it can leave watermarks.

For heavy bridal blouses, professional steam pressing is the safest choice.

## What about the saree itself?

The same principles apply: air after wearing, dry clean, wrap in cotton and refold regularly. Keep the matching blouse with its saree so they age together and stay easy to find.

## When should you bring a blouse for repair?

Bring it in if you notice loose stones, broken threads in embroidery, a hook coming off, or a seam starting to open. Small repairs done early are quick. Left alone, a loose thread can unravel a whole section of aari work.
