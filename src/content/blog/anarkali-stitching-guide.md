An anarkali is one of the most graceful outfits you can stitch, and one of the easiest to get slightly wrong. If the bodice is loose, the skirt drops unevenly. If there is too much flare for a soft fabric, it collapses; too little in a stiff fabric, and it looks like a tent. Here is how we plan an anarkali, so you know what to ask for.

## How is an anarkali constructed?

An anarkali has two parts:

1. **The bodice (choli).** Fitted closely at the bust and waist, often with princess seams or darts.
2. **The skirt (kalis).** Made of triangular or tapered panels called kalis, joined at the waist or under the bust, flaring out to the hem.

The seam where they meet is the most important line on the garment. If it sits in the right place on your body, the whole anarkali falls correctly.

## How many kalis and how much flare?

| Flare level | Kalis | Look | Suits | Fabric used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle | Fewer, wider panels | A-line, everyday | Office, daily, petite frames | Least |
| Medium | Moderate count | Graceful twirl | Festivals, family functions | Moderate |
| Full | Many narrow panels | Dramatic, festive | Weddings, sangeet, taller frames | Most |
| Double-layer | Two skirts, often net over silk | Very rich | Receptions | Highest |

More kalis mean more seams, so they also mean more stitching time and more fabric. Heavy embroidery along each kali seam adds further work.

## Which fabrics suit an anarkali?

- **Georgette and chiffon:** the classic choice; they flow beautifully but need a lining.
- **Cotton and mul:** good for daily anarkalis with gentle flare; too stiff for very full flare unless soft.
- **Silk and silk-cotton:** hold a structured flare, lovely for temple functions.
- **Net:** used as an outer layer over silk or satin for a festive look.
- **Crepe:** drapes softly and does not crease easily.

The [blouse fabric guide](/blog/blouse-fabric-guide/) explains how fabric weight affects stitching; the same principles apply here.

## How much fabric does an anarkali need?

An anarkali needs much more fabric than a straight kurti because each kali is cut separately. The exact amount depends on your height, the length you want, the number of kalis and the fabric width. Our [salwar suit fabric guide](/blog/how-much-fabric-for-salwar-suit/) gives typical ranges by style and height. When in doubt, buy a little extra; you can always use leftovers for the dupatta piping or sleeves.

## Should an anarkali be lined?

Usually yes. The lining:

- Helps the kalis hang evenly instead of clinging.
- Stops sheer fabrics from being see-through.
- Covers the many internal seams so they do not scratch.

Cotton lining is the coolest choice in warm weather. Crepe drapes more softly for flowing fabrics. For a fuller skirt, a separate can-can or extra layer can be added at the hem. See the [lining fabric guide](/blog/lining-fabric-guide/) for more.

## What are the key fitting points?

When we take measurements for an anarkali, these matter most:

- **Bust and under-bust:** decide where the bodice ends.
- **Waist:** must fit snugly so the skirt does not drop.
- **Shoulder to waist length:** sets where the joining seam sits.
- **Full length:** measured with the footwear you plan to wear.
- **Armhole and sleeve:** important if you choose fitted full sleeves.

At the trial, check that the joining seam sits level all around, the bodice does not wrinkle when you raise your arms, and the hem is even at the front and back.

## Which necklines and sleeves suit an anarkali?

The neckline balances the volume of the skirt. A [sweetheart neck](/designs/sweetheart-neck/), round neck or V neck works well. Sleeves can be full and sheer for weddings or elbow-length for daily wear. A [full sleeve](/designs/full-sleeve/) in net with a little embroidery at the cuff is a classic festive combination.

## Common anarkali problems and fixes

- **Skirt dips at the front:** the bodice is too long or the bust measurement was off.
- **Flare collapses:** too many kalis for a soft fabric, or no lining.
- **Bodice gapes at the neck:** neckline cut too wide for a soft fabric.
- **Hem uneven:** fabric was not allowed to hang before hemming; bias-cut kalis stretch.
