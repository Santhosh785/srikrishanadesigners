Your bridal outfits are made once, worn once in the moment that matters, and photographed forever. Choosing the right tailor is as important as choosing the fabric. This guide gives you a checklist to use when visiting studios, the red flags we hear about from brides who come to us after a bad experience, and a timeline to work back from.

## What should you look for in a bridal tailor?

A good bridal tailor is not just skilled; they are organised and honest about time. Look for these signs:

- **Their own work.** They can show you finished pieces or photos of garments made in their studio, not images taken from the internet.
- **A clear quote.** They give you a written price for each outfit, including what is and is not included.
- **A real delivery date.** They give a specific date based on their current workload, not a vague "it will be ready".
- **Trial fittings.** They plan at least two fittings for bridal outfits.
- **Fabric care.** They explain how they will handle expensive silks and embroidered pieces, and where they will be stored.
- **Questions back to you.** A good tailor asks about your function dates, footwear, innerwear and comfort, not just your measurements.

## What questions should you ask before booking?

1. How many bridal orders are you taking for my wedding month?
2. When will my first and second trial fittings be?
3. Is the embroidery done in-house or sent out?
4. What is included in the price: lining, padding, can-can, latkans, dupatta finishing?
5. How much advance do you need, and when is the balance due?
6. What happens if the outfit does not fit at the final fitting?
7. Can you leave extra seam allowance in case my weight changes?

Write down the answers. Comparing studios becomes much easier when you have the same information from each. Our [blouse cost estimator](/tools/blouse-cost-estimator/) shows what typically drives the price.

## What are the red flags?

| Red flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Promises heavy aari work in a few days | Rushed hand work shows; or the work is being outsourced without your knowledge |
| No written quote or receipt | Prices can change at delivery; no record of your fabric |
| No trial fitting offered | Fit problems are found only on the day, when it is too late |
| Cannot show their own past work | You cannot judge quality before handing over your fabric |
| Takes every order without checking dates | Likely to be overloaded during the wedding season |
| Dismisses your comfort concerns | You will be wearing the outfit for many hours |

One red flag is not always a dealbreaker, but two or three together are a reason to keep looking.

## What timeline should you follow?

For a wedding with embroidered outfits, work back from the first function:

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 10–12 weeks before | Shortlist tailors, buy fabrics, collect design references |
| 8 weeks before | Book the tailor, give measurements and fabric, agree quotes |
| 6–7 weeks before | Hand embroidery begins |
| 4 weeks before | First trial fitting |
| 2 weeks before | Second trial fitting with embroidery done |
| 1 week before | Final fit check and pickup |

If your timeline is shorter, talk honestly with the tailor about simplifying the embroidery. Our post on [how long an aari work blouse takes](/blog/how-long-does-aari-work-blouse-take/) explains what can and cannot be sped up.

## How do you prepare for your first meeting?

- Bring every fabric piece for each outfit, including dupattas.
- Bring photos of the designs you like, front and back.
- Know your function dates and which outfit is for which function.
- Wear or bring the innerwear you plan to use.
- Bring the footwear, or at least know the heel height, for lehengas.
- Bring a well-fitting blouse as a reference.

## What should you check at each trial fitting?

- Raise both arms. The blouse should not ride up much or pull at the armhole.
- Sit down. The waist and hips should not feel tight.
- Walk a few steps. The lehenga hem should not catch your feet.
- Look at the back in a mirror. Necklines should sit flat, without gaping.
- Check the embroidery placement on the sleeves matches on both sides.

Speak up at the trial. It is far easier to correct something then than a few days before the wedding.
