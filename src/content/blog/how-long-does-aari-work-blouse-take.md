Aari work is the hand embroidery you see on most South Indian bridal and festive blouses: fine chain stitches, zari outlines, stones, sequins and beads worked into peacocks, mangoes, temple motifs and floral creepers. Because every stitch is done by hand, the most common question we get is not about price but about time. Here is how long it really takes, and why.

## How long does an aari work blouse take?

For most orders, plan on **2–3 weeks** from the day you hand over the fabric to the day you take the blouse home. The embroidery itself may take a few days or a couple of weeks depending on the design, and the rest of the time goes into marking, stitching, a trial fitting and finishing.

| Design level | Typical embroidery coverage | Approximate total time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light | Small buttas on sleeves, thin neckline border | 7–10 days |
| Medium | Sleeve borders plus a back neck design | 2 weeks |
| Heavy | Full sleeves and back with stones and beads | 3 weeks |
| Bridal | Dense coverage on sleeves, back and front, figures or temple motifs | 3–4 weeks or more |

These are typical timelines outside peak season. In the months with many weddings, queues get longer, so add at least a week.

## Why does aari work take so long?

### It is done stitch by stitch

The embroiderer pushes a hooked needle through fabric stretched on a wooden frame, pulling thread up from below to form a chain. Filling a single mango motif with zari and outlining it with stones can take hours. A dense bridal back is made of dozens of such motifs.

### The work follows a sequence

An aari blouse is not made in one sitting. The usual order is:

1. **Measurements** are taken, because the design must fit your sleeve width and back neck size.
2. **The design is traced** onto the fabric pieces using a paper stencil and chalk or powder.
3. **The fabric is framed**, with each piece stretched tight.
4. **The embroidery is done**, starting with outlines, then fillings, then stones and beads.
5. **The pieces are removed from the frame** and checked for loose threads.
6. **The blouse is stitched**, with lining, and any padding or closures.
7. **Trial fitting**, followed by final finishing and pressing.

Each step depends on the one before it, so the work cannot be split up and done all at once.

### Materials sometimes need to be sourced

If your design needs a specific colour of zari, a particular size of kundan stone or matching beads, they may have to be bought before work begins. This is one reason studios ask for an advance.

## What makes an aari blouse take longer?

- **Coverage.** The more of the blouse that is embroidered, the longer it takes.
- **Fine detail.** Figures, faces and intricate temple patterns take longer than repeating floral buttas.
- **Heavy embellishment.** Stones, cut-dana and beads are attached one by one.
- **Delicate fabric.** Soft silks and tissue fabrics have to be handled carefully on the frame.
- **Changes after work has started.** Changing the design midway can mean unpicking finished embroidery.

## Can an aari blouse be rushed?

Only partly. A studio can sometimes put more than one embroiderer on a design or prioritise your order, but there is a limit. Rushed hand work tends to show in uneven stitches, loose stones and thread pulls on the fabric. If your date is close, it is usually better to choose a lighter design that can be done properly than a heavy design done in a hurry.

Some alternatives when time is short:

- Embroider only the sleeves and keep the back plain, or add a simple dori.
- Use a ready embroidered border or patch for the back and add light hand work around it.
- Choose machine embroidery for one of the less important function blouses.

## When should you order an aari blouse for a wedding?

Work backwards from the function date:

- **Bridal blouses:** order 6–8 weeks before the wedding, so there is room for two fittings.
- **Family and guest blouses with medium work:** order 3–4 weeks before.
- **Light sleeve work for a festival:** order 2 weeks before.

## How should you care for an aari blouse after delivery?

Hand embroidery lasts for years if it is treated gently. Dry clean only, do not wring it, and store it folded in a muslin or cotton cloth with the embroidered side inside. Our post on [silk saree blouse care](/blog/silk-saree-blouse-care/) has detailed steps.
