A trial fitting is your best chance to catch a fit problem while it can still be fixed. Many brides walk in, see the blouse looks lovely in the mirror, and say it is fine. Then on the wedding day the armhole pinches or the back gapes. Use this checklist to make each trial count.

## Why does a bridal blouse need two trials?

A bridal blouse is fitted twice because the embroidery changes how it sits.

- **First trial:** the base blouse, often in plain stitched or tacked form, before the embroidery is finished or attached. This is where the basic fit is fixed.
- **Second trial:** after embroidery. Heavy work adds weight and stiffness, so the blouse needs checking again.

Our [bridal blouse planning guide](/blog/bridal-blouse-planning-guide/) shows where these trials fall in the overall timeline.

## What should you bring to the trial?

- The innerwear you will wear on the day, or a padded preference confirmed.
- Your saree or lehenga and dupatta.
- Main jewellery, especially necklaces and long haram, to check the neck depth.
- Footwear for lehengas, so the length can be checked.
- Your original design photos.
- Someone who can look at the back and take photos.

## What should you check at the first trial?

| Area | What to check | Sign of a problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bust point | Shaping sits at your bust point | Wrinkles above or below the bust |
| Shoulder seam | Sits on the shoulder tip | Seam slides forward or drops down the arm |
| Armhole | Comfortable, no cutting in | Pinching or a gap at the armpit |
| Front neck | Depth and width as planned | Neck gaping or too open with jewellery |
| Back neck | Sits flat | Gaping, or showing bra straps |
| Under-bust | Snug but comfortable | Riding up or digging in |
| Length | Ends where planned | Too short when arms raised |
| Sleeve | Length and round are right | Tight at the arm when bent |

## How do you test comfort, not just looks?

Stand still and the blouse may look perfect. Then do these:

1. **Raise both arms** as if putting on a garland. The blouse should not ride up much.
2. **Sit down** on a chair and on the floor if your ceremony involves sitting.
3. **Bend forward** slightly, as for a namaskaram. Check the front neck.
4. **Turn and twist** to check the back and sides.
5. **Breathe deeply.** A blouse you will wear for many hours should not feel tight after a meal.

## What should you check at the second trial?

Everything from the first trial, plus:

- **Embroidery symmetry:** both sleeves match, motifs centred on the back.
- **Placement with the saree border:** sleeve borders line up with the saree.
- **Weight:** heavy work on the sleeves should not pull the shoulder seam.
- **Closures:** hooks, buttons or zip open and close easily; a [dori back](/designs/dori-back/) or [potli button back](/designs/potli-button-back/) sits neatly.
- **Inside finishing:** seams neat, no rough embroidery backs against the skin.
- **Lining:** smooth, not pulling against the outer fabric.

## How do you give clear feedback to your tailor?

- Point to the exact spot and describe what you feel, like "pinches here when I lift my arm".
- Compare to the design photo if something looks different.
- Ask what can and cannot be changed at this stage.
- Note the agreed changes and the date of the next fitting.

## What are the most common things brides forget to check?

From our fittings, these are the points most often missed until the last minute:

- **The jewellery test.** A heavy necklace can sit on the neckline and push it down. Wear it at the trial.
- **The pallu test.** Drape the pallu over the shoulder; heavy silk can drag a loose shoulder seam backward.
- **Getting in and out.** Can you open the blouse without help? For a dori or back-hook closure, plan who will help you on the day.
- **Long hours.** Ceremonies can run for hours. A blouse that feels fine for five minutes may not be fine after a long morning.
- **Photographs from the side and back.** Ask someone to take photos from all angles at the trial, and look at them later in good light.

## What if your weight changes between trials?

Tell your tailor early. Ask for extra seam allowance at the side seams if you expect changes. Our [blouse measurement guide](/blog/blouse-measurement-guide/) explains which measurements matter most.
