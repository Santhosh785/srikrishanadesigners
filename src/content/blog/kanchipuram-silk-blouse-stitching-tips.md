A Kanchipuram silk saree is often the most treasured saree a woman owns, and the blouse decides how finished it looks. Heavy pattu silk with a wide zari border behaves very differently from light silk or cotton. It is stiff, it does not ease into curves, and the border can only be used once. A little planning before the scissors come out makes the difference.

## What makes Kanchipuram silk harder to stitch?

- **Weight and stiffness.** Pure pattu has body. That is good for structure, but it means tight curves and gathers do not sit softly.
- **Zari borders.** Metallic zari cannot be stretched or eased; a border cut in the wrong place is lost.
- **Fraying.** Silk edges fray, especially near zari, so seams need neat finishing.
- **Colour.** Rich dyes can bleed if the blouse gets wet, so pre-washing silk is not advised.
- **Limited fabric.** The attached blouse piece is usually just enough, with little room for mistakes.

## How should the zari border be used?

Before cutting, we lay the blouse piece flat and measure the usable border length. Then we choose one of these layouts:

| Border placement | Look | Fabric needed | Works with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sleeve hems only | Classic, matches saree border | Least border | Elbow or short sleeves |
| Sleeves and back neck | Grand, good in photos | Medium | Boat, round or square back |
| Front neckline | Traditional, frames the face | Medium; straight neck shapes only | Square or boat neck |
| Border running down the back opening | Unusual, elegant | Long border strip | Back-open blouses |

Straight necklines suit zari borders best, because the border stays flat. A [boat neck](/designs/boat-neck/) or square neck can carry a border cleanly; a deep U or sweetheart shape would force the zari to curve and buckle.

## Which sleeves suit heavy silk?

- [Elbow sleeves](/designs/elbow-sleeve/) are the most popular choice; they show the full border and are comfortable for long ceremonies.
- Short sleeves suit younger brides and warm afternoon functions.
- Three-quarter sleeves look regal for older women or temple weddings.
- Puff sleeves are possible but need a soft lining and careful gathering, because pattu resists gathers.

Keep the sleeve fitted but not tight. Silk does not stretch, and a tight sleeve on a heavy blouse becomes uncomfortable within an hour.

## How do you match the pallu?

The pallu often has the saree's richest design. Ways to bring it into the blouse:

1. **Use a pallu motif on the back.** If the saree has extra pallu length, a motif can be cut and placed on the back of the blouse.
2. **Pick the pallu colour.** Many Kanchipuram sarees have a contrast pallu. A blouse in that colour ties the drape together.
3. **Repeat the border pattern.** Use leftover border pieces as a patch at the back or as sleeve bands.

Never cut the saree itself without being completely sure; we will always confirm the plan with you first.

## When is a contrast blouse a good idea?

A contrast blouse in plain silk, raw silk or brocade makes sense when:

- The attached blouse piece is too short or does not match well.
- You want to wear the saree in different ways for different functions.
- You plan heavy aari work, which shows better on a plain fabric. See our comparison of [aari vs maggam work](/blog/aari-vs-maggam-work/).

Choose a contrast colour from the saree's border or pallu so it looks deliberate.

## Stitching tips for a Kanchipuram blouse

- Line it in soft, pre-washed cotton.
- Use strong hooks or a concealed zip on the back if it is heavily embroidered.
- Finish seams neatly to stop silk fraying near the zari.
- Keep extra seam allowance at the sides so the blouse can be altered later.
- Press from the reverse with a cloth in between; never press directly on zari.
