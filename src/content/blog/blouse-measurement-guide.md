A blouse is the most closely fitted garment most Indian women wear, and even half an inch in the wrong place shows. Whether you are sending measurements to your tailor or just want to understand what they are measuring, this guide explains each measurement, how to take it, and the mistakes that cause most fitting problems.

## What is the easiest way to give blouse measurements?

**Give your tailor a blouse that already fits you well.** A good tailor can take more than a dozen measurements from a sample, and it also shows them the ease you are comfortable with, which is hard to describe in numbers. If you are trying a new style such as princess cut or padded, the sample still helps as a baseline.

Choose a sample that:

- Fits comfortably at the bust, armhole and shoulder.
- Is not stretched out from many washes.
- Is a similar style to what you want, if possible.
- Was worn recently, so it reflects your current size.

## What do you need to measure at home?

You need a soft measuring tape, a mirror, and ideally someone to help. Wear the innerwear you will wear with the blouse, stand straight and breathe normally.

| Measurement | How to take it | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bust | Around the fullest part of the bust, tape level all round | Tape dipping at the back |
| Under-bust | Just below the bust, where the blouse band sits | Pulling the tape too tight |
| Waist (blouse) | Where the blouse ends, usually 2–3 inches below the under-bust | Measuring the trouser waist instead |
| Shoulder | From one shoulder tip to the other across the back | Measuring over the neck curve |
| Armhole | Around the arm, through the armpit and over the shoulder | Measuring too loosely |
| Sleeve length | From shoulder tip to where you want the sleeve to end | Forgetting to decide elbow vs short |
| Sleeve round | Around the arm at the point where the sleeve ends | Measuring with arm tensed |
| Front neck depth | From the shoulder line down to where the neckline should sit | Choosing a depth without checking innerwear |
| Back neck depth | From the base of the neck down to the back neckline | Going deeper than the bra strap allows |
| Blouse length | From the shoulder over the bust to the desired hem | Measuring straight down instead of over the bust |

For princess-cut and padded blouses, the tailor also needs **shoulder to bust point** and **distance between bust points**. These are best taken in person.

## How do you take each measurement correctly?

### Bust and under-bust

Hold the tape snug but not tight; you should be able to slide one finger under it. Look in the mirror to check that the tape is horizontal all the way around. A tape that rises at the back gives a smaller number than your true size.

### Shoulder

Find the bony tip of each shoulder. Measure straight across the back from one tip to the other. This decides where the sleeve seam sits, so a wrong shoulder measurement makes the whole blouse look off.

### Armhole

This is the measurement most often responsible for discomfort. Wrap the tape around the top of the arm, passing under the armpit and over the shoulder tip. Keep it close but not tight. A tight armhole restricts movement; a loose one causes gaping and wrinkles.

### Neck depths

Decide neck depth with your innerwear on. Measure from the top of the shoulder (where a bag strap would sit) straight down to where you want the neckline. For the back, make sure the depth sits above or below your bra strap, never right on it.

## What are the most common measurement mistakes?

1. **Measuring over clothes.** Even a thin T-shirt adds bulk.
2. **Using an old stretched tape.** Check it against a ruler.
3. **Holding your breath or slouching.** Stand naturally.
4. **Rounding numbers.** Write down half-inches; they matter in a blouse.
5. **Measuring yourself alone.** Bust and shoulder measurements are hard to take on your own body.
6. **Not mentioning upcoming changes.** If you are losing weight for a wedding, tell your tailor.

## Should you still go for a trial fitting?

For a simple blouse copied from a good sample, a trial is optional. For princess-cut, padded, bridal or heavily embroidered blouses, a trial fitting is worth the trip. It catches issues like shoulder slope, posture and bust point placement that no measurement chart can show.
