From March to June, a blouse that felt fine in December can feel like a jacket. Cotton is the obvious answer for summer, but the cut matters as much as the fabric. A cotton blouse with a tight armhole, satin lining and padding will still be hot. This guide covers fabric, cut and care for a blouse you can wear comfortably through a warm day.

## Which cotton fabric is best for a summer blouse?

| Fabric | Weight | Feel | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mul cotton | Very light | Soft, slightly sheer | Daily wear, home, light functions |
| Cotton voile | Light | Smooth, fine | Printed sarees, office |
| Handloom cotton | Medium | Textured, crisp | Office, handloom sarees |
| Khadi | Medium | Breathable, textured | Office, casual |
| Kalamkari or block-printed cotton | Medium | Crisp | Contrast blouses for plain sarees |
| Linen or linen-cotton | Medium | Cool, creases easily | Linen sarees, office |

For Coimbatore summers, handloom and mul cottons are reliable. Linen is cool but creases quickly, which some people like and some do not.

## What cut keeps a blouse cool?

- **Easy armholes.** A slightly deeper, well-shaped armhole lets air move and stops sweat patches building up.
- **Close, not tight, fit.** A tight blouse traps heat. Ask for a little ease at the bust and back.
- **No padding.** Padding is warm. A well-cut darted blouse supports without it. See our post on [padded vs non-padded blouses](/blog/padded-vs-non-padded-blouse/).
- **Back opening or front opening by preference.** Front hooks are easier to manage on busy mornings.

## Which necklines suit summer?

Open necklines feel cooler and look fresh with cotton sarees:

- [Boat neck](/designs/boat-neck/): covers the front but opens across the collarbone; looks neat with handloom.
- Round neck: the simplest everyday option.
- Square neck: smart for office and photographs well.
- U neck: a little deeper, airy, good for evening wear.

High necks and Chinese collars look smart but feel warmer; save them for air-conditioned offices or cooler months.

## Which sleeves are coolest?

- [Cap sleeves](/designs/cap-sleeve/) and short sleeves let the most air in while covering the shoulder.
- Elbow sleeves, cut slightly loose, are a good balance for office wear and sun protection.
- Sleeveless is coolest but not everyone is comfortable with it at work.
- Avoid tight full sleeves in summer unless the fabric is very light.

## Should a cotton blouse be lined?

Mostly no. A medium-weight cotton blouse is opaque enough on its own and stays cooler without a lining. If the cotton is thin, light-coloured, or the blouse has embroidery, use a very thin pre-washed cotton lining, never polyester satin. Our [lining fabric guide](/blog/lining-fabric-guide/) explains why.

## How do you care for cotton blouses?

1. **Pre-wash before stitching** to avoid shrinkage.
2. **Wash in cold water**, inside out, separately the first few times in case the colour runs.
3. **Dry in shade**; strong sun fades handloom and printed cotton.
4. **Iron while slightly damp** for crisp results.
5. **Starch lightly** if you like a sharper look for office.

Sweat stains are the biggest enemy in summer. Wash the blouse soon after wearing rather than leaving it in a laundry pile.

## Which colours and prints work best in the heat?

Light colours reflect heat and show less sweat at the underarms than deep reds or dark blues, though they can show stains more. Small prints, checks and handloom textures hide creases through the day. If you prefer dark colours, choose a thicker handloom so sweat marks are less visible.

A contrast blouse is an easy way to refresh cotton sarees. A plain white, black or mustard cotton blouse, or a kalamkari print, can be paired with several sarees, so a few well-stitched blouses go a long way.

## A summer blouse checklist

- Fabric pre-washed and ironed
- No padding, no satin lining
- Comfortable armholes
- Open neckline
- Short, cap or loose elbow sleeves
- Hooks placed where you can manage them easily
