A bridal blouse is not one garment; it is a small project with fabric, design, embroidery, fittings and deadlines that all depend on each other. Most last-minute problems we see come from starting one step too late, not from bad tailoring. This guide gives you a month-by-month plan, a way to think about budget, and what each trial fitting is for.

The fastest way to build your own schedule is our [bridal timeline planner](/tools/bridal-timeline-planner/). Enter your function dates and it works back to the dates you need to buy fabric, book your tailor and attend trials.

## When should you start planning your bridal blouse?

Start **three to four months** before your first function if your blouses will have aari, maggam or other hand embroidery. Hand work cannot be rushed without showing it, and the best embroiderers fill up first during muhurtham months. If your outfits are simpler, two months can work, but you lose flexibility.

## What does a month-by-month bridal blouse plan look like?

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 4 months before | List every function and which saree or lehenga goes with each. Collect design photos, front and back. |
| 3–4 months | Buy sarees and any extra blouse fabric or lining. Shortlist tailors and visit with your fabric. |
| 3 months | Book your tailor, finalise designs, give measurements, agree written quotes and delivery dates. |
| 2–3 months | Embroidery begins on the heaviest blouse first, usually the muhurtham blouse. |
| 6 weeks | First trial fitting of the base blouse. Fix fit before embroidery is completed or attached. |
| 3–4 weeks | Embroidery finishes. Simpler function blouses are stitched. |
| 2 weeks | Second trial fitting with embroidery done. Check sleeves, back and closures. |
| 1 week | Final pickup. Try everything on once more at home with jewellery and innerwear. |

Your exact dates will differ, so use the [bridal timeline planner](/tools/bridal-timeline-planner/) rather than counting weeks on a calendar.

## How do you decide what each function needs?

Not every blouse needs to be bridal-heavy. A common way to plan:

- **Muhurtham** — the heaviest work, usually on a Kanchipuram or silk saree, with elbow or three-quarter sleeves.
- **Engagement / nichayathartham** — rich but lighter, often in a contrast silk.
- **Reception** — more modern: sweetheart or high necks, sheer sleeves, or a lehenga. See [lehenga vs saree for reception](/blog/lehenga-vs-saree-for-reception/).
- **Mehendi, haldi, post-wedding** — lighter fabrics, simpler work, comfortable fits.

## How should you budget for bridal blouses?

We do not quote fixed figures here, because the price depends on the design, the amount of hand work and the fabric. What we can share is how the budget usually divides:

1. **Embroidery is the biggest factor.** Coverage area (sleeves only, sleeves and back, or full blouse) matters more than the motif itself.
2. **Type of work.** Aari and maggam are priced differently; see [aari vs maggam work](/blog/aari-vs-maggam-work/).
3. **Cut and structure.** Princess cut, padding and lining add a little each.
4. **Rush timelines.** Compressing hand work usually costs more and risks quality.

Practical budgeting tips:

- Give the largest share to the muhurtham blouse, which is photographed most.
- Use lighter work on blouses for secondary functions.
- Keep a buffer for alterations, latkans, extra lining and last-minute fixes.
- Get every quote in writing and compare like for like.

## What happens at each trial fitting?

**First trial** — the base blouse, before embroidery is completed or attached. This is where fit is fixed: bust point, shoulder seam, armhole, neck depth and length. Bring the innerwear you will wear on the day.

**Second trial** — with embroidery done. Now you check that the work is placed symmetrically, the sleeves sit evenly, closures work and the blouse is comfortable when you sit and raise your arms.

Our [bridal blouse trial fitting checklist](/blog/bridal-blouse-trial-fitting-checklist/) lists exactly what to check at each one.

## What are the most common planning mistakes?

- Buying the saree late and leaving no time for embroidery.
- Choosing a design from a photo without checking it suits the saree's border.
- Skipping the first trial to save a trip.
- Losing or gaining weight after measurements without telling the tailor.
- Collecting outfits the day before the function with no time to fix anything.

## What should you keep track of during planning?

With four or five outfits moving at once, a simple tracker helps. For each blouse, note:

| Item | Example entry |
| --- | --- |
| Function and date | Muhurtham, first Sunday of Thai |
| Saree or lehenga | Kanchipuram silk, maroon with gold border |
| Design | Paan neck, elbow sleeves, potli button back |
| Work | Aari on sleeves and back |
| Quote and advance | Written quote received, advance paid |
| Trial dates | First and second trial dates agreed |
| Pickup date | One week before the function |

Keep the receipt for your fabric and a photo of each saree before you hand it over. If your family is ordering blouses for the mother of the bride or bridesmaids, add them to the same tracker so their dates do not clash with yours.
