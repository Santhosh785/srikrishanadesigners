A salwar suit is the most versatile outfit in a South Indian woman's wardrobe. The same fabric can become an office kurti, a festive anarkali or a relaxed kaftan, depending on the cut. Getting it stitched lets you choose the style, length and bottom that suit your body and your day, instead of settling for what a shop has in your size. This guide walks through the main styles and how to choose.

## What are the main kurti styles?

| Kurti style | Shape | Best occasions | Flatters | Fabric needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight | Straight from bust to hem, side slits | Office, daily, college | Most figures; slim and average builds especially | Least |
| A-line | Fitted at bust, widens gently to hem | Office, daily, light festive | Pear shapes, fuller hips, petite | Slightly more |
| Anarkali | Fitted bodice, full flared skirt from the waist or under-bust | Festivals, weddings, receptions | Most figures; defines the waist | Most |
| Kaftan | Loose, wide, often with a drawstring or belt | Home, travel, casual outings | Fuller figures, anyone wanting comfort | Moderate |
| Pakistani straight | Long, straight, calf length or longer, high slits | Weddings, evening functions | Taller women; creates a long line | Moderate to high |
| Princess-cut kurti | Seams from bust shaping the waist | Office, festive | Hourglass and average figures | Moderate |

## What bottoms go with each kurti?

- **Churidar:** fitted from knee to ankle, best with knee-length and longer kurtis and anarkalis. For fitting details, see [salwar vs churidar](/blog/salwar-vs-churidar-fitting-guide/).
- **Straight pants or cigarette pants:** modern and practical; good with straight and A-line kurtis for office.
- **Palazzo:** wide and flowing; balances short and straight kurtis, very cool in summer.
- **Salwar or Patiala:** relaxed and festive; best with shorter kurtis so the pleats show.
- **Sharara or gharara:** flared or tiered bottoms for festive and wedding wear.

A simple rule: pair volume with slimness. A flared kurti looks best over a slim bottom, and a straight kurti can take a wider bottom.

## How do you choose a kurti style by body type?

### Fuller bust

Straight or A-line kurtis with a V neck, U neck or sweetheart neck draw the eye downward. Avoid high necks with heavy embroidery on the chest. Princess seams help shape the fit without pulling.

### Fuller hips or pear shape

A-line and anarkali cuts skim over the hips. Add interest at the neckline or sleeves to balance the shape. Keep bottoms slim, such as churidar or straight pants.

### Petite

Knee-length kurtis, vertical details like a placket or front buttons, and narrow bottoms all add height. Keep anarkalis to mid-calf rather than ankle length.

### Tall

Most styles work. Pakistani straight suits and floor-length anarkalis look particularly good. Palazzos and shararas suit taller frames well.

### Broad shoulders

Raglan or slightly dropped shoulders, V necks and A-line cuts balance the shoulders. Avoid boat necks and puffed sleeves.

### Fuller arms

[Three-quarter sleeves](/designs/three-quarter-sleeve/) or elbow-length sleeves with a little ease are comfortable and flattering. Avoid tight sleeves that pull when you lift your arms.

## Which style suits which occasion?

- **Office:** straight or A-line, cotton or linen, knee length, practical neckline. Our post on [kurti neck designs for office](/blog/kurti-neck-designs-for-office/) has ideas.
- **Daily and college:** straight, A-line or kaftan in cotton, with palazzos or pants.
- **Temple visits and family functions:** A-line or straight in silk-cotton, with churidar.
- **Festivals:** anarkali or a long straight kurti with embroidery.
- **Weddings and receptions:** floor-length anarkali, Pakistani straight suit or kurti with sharara.

## How does kurti length change the look?

- **Hip length:** casual and modern, pairs with pants and palazzos.
- **Knee length:** the most versatile; works with almost every bottom.
- **Calf length:** elegant, pairs best with churidar or straight pants.
- **Floor length:** festive, usually an anarkali or gown-style kurti.

When you tell us the length, also tell us the footwear you will wear, especially for floor-length styles.

## How do neck and sleeve choices change a kurti?

Necklines that work on blouses also work on kurtis, with slightly higher depths for daily wear. Collars and keyhole necks look smart for office; round and boat necks are classic; V and sweetheart necks flatter fuller busts. You can browse necklines and sleeves in the [design finder](/designs/) and use them for kurtis too.

## Should you stitch or buy ready-made?

Ready-made kurtis are convenient, but they are cut for a standard size chart. If your bust and hip fall in different sizes, if you are taller or shorter than average, or if you want a specific neckline or sleeve length, stitching gives a better result. It also lets you:

- Choose the exact length for your height and footwear.
- Add practical details like side pockets or a front placket.
- Keep extra seam allowance so the kurti can be altered later.
- Use fabric you already own, such as a saree you no longer drape.

Many customers bring one ready-made kurti that fits well and ask us to copy its fit in new fabric. That is a reliable way to start.
