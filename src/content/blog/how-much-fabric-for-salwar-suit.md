Buying fabric for a salwar suit by the metre is cheaper and gives you more choice than buying a ready-made set, but it only works if you buy enough. Too little and the tailor has to drop the sleeves or shorten the kurti; too much is wasted money. This guide gives typical fabric ranges by style and height so you can buy with confidence.

## How much fabric do you need for each piece?

These are typical ranges for fabric about 42 to 44 inches wide. They are a starting point, not a guarantee; the exact amount depends on your measurements, sleeve length and design.

| Piece | Petite (under about 5'2") | Average (about 5'2" to 5'6") | Tall (over about 5'6") |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short kurti (hip length) | 1.5 m | 1.75 m | 2 m |
| Straight kurti (knee length) | 2 m | 2.25–2.5 m | 2.75 m |
| A-line kurti (knee length) | 2.25 m | 2.5–2.75 m | 3 m |
| Pakistani straight (calf length or longer) | 2.75 m | 3 m | 3.25–3.5 m |
| Anarkali, gentle flare | 3.5 m | 4 m | 4.5 m |
| Anarkali, full flare (floor length) | 4.5 m | 5–5.5 m | 6 m or more |
| Salwar | 2 m | 2.25 m | 2.5 m |
| Churidar | 2 m | 2.25–2.5 m | 2.75 m |
| Straight pants | 1.5 m | 1.75 m | 2 m |
| Palazzo | 2.25 m | 2.5 m | 3 m |
| Patiala salwar | 2.75 m | 3 m | 3.5 m |
| Dupatta | 2.25 m | 2.5 m | 2.5–2.75 m |

## What changes the amount of fabric?

- **Height and length.** The biggest factor. Every extra inch of kurti length is taken twice, front and back.
- **Bust and hip measurement.** If your hip measurement is more than the fabric width allows for one front and one back side by side, the tailor has to cut pieces one below the other, which uses more length.
- **Sleeves.** Full sleeves need more than three-quarter or elbow sleeves. Sleeveless saves fabric.
- **Flare.** A-line and anarkali cuts need much more than straight cuts. The [anarkali stitching guide](/blog/anarkali-stitching-guide/) explains how the number of kalis affects this.
- **Fabric width.** Narrow handlooms need more length; wide fabrics need less.
- **Prints and borders.** Large prints, checks and border fabrics need extra to match patterns and place borders at the hem.
- **Shrinkage.** Cotton and rayon shrink, so buy a little extra and pre-wash.

## How should you measure before buying?

Taking two or three measurements at home helps you use the table above more accurately:

1. **Kurti length:** from the highest point of the shoulder down to where you want the hem.
2. **Hip:** around the fullest part of the hips.
3. **Bottom length:** from the waist to the ankle, wearing the footwear you plan to use.

Write these on our [measurement card](/tools/blouse-measurement-card/) so you have them with you at the fabric shop. The [blouse measurement guide](/blog/blouse-measurement-guide/) explains how to take upper-body measurements accurately.

## Tips for buying fabric for a salwar suit

- **Buy all pieces in one go** if they need to match, since dye lots vary.
- **Check the width** printed on the bolt or ask the shop. Do not assume.
- **Add extra for prints** that need matching at the centre front.
- **Pre-wash cotton** before stitching; see our [lining fabric guide](/blog/lining-fabric-guide/) for how.
- **Lining is separate.** Sheer kurtis and anarkalis need lining fabric too, roughly the same length as the kurti or bodice and skirt.

## What if you have already bought the fabric?

Bring it in. Often a slightly short piece can still work with a small change: elbow sleeves instead of full, a contrast yoke, a slightly shorter length, or a contrast fabric for the bottom. We will tell you what is possible before cutting.
