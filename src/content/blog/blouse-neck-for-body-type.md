There are no wrong bodies, only necklines that do or do not work with a particular frame. The aim is balance: draw the eye where you want it and avoid lines that cut you at an unflattering point. These are the pairings we suggest most often at fittings. Use them as a starting point, not a rule book, and remember that the right fit matters more than any style rule.

## Which necks and sleeves suit each body type?

| Body note | Necks that work | Sleeves that work | Go easy on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Broad shoulders | V-neck, sweetheart | Elbow, three-quarter | Wide boat necks, big puff sleeves |
| Narrow shoulders | Boat neck, square neck | Puff, frill | Deep, narrow V |
| Short neck | Deep V, U-neck | Any | High neck, Chinese collar |
| Long neck | High neck, Chinese collar | Any | Very deep, wide necks |
| Fuller bust | V, sweetheart, deeper U | Elbow | High necks, heavy work across the bust |
| Petite | Round, U, smaller V | Cap, elbow | Very wide necks, heavy sleeves |
| Fuller arms | Any | Elbow, three-quarter, sheer net | Tight cap sleeves |
| Slim arms | Any | Puff, cap, bell | — |

## What suits broad shoulders?

Necks that draw the eye toward the centre break up shoulder width. A [V-neck](/designs/v-neck/) does this best, and a sweetheart neck does it with a softer curve. Keep sleeves simple and let them end at or below the elbow, so the line continues down the arm rather than stopping at the widest point.

For the back, a keyhole or dori back keeps the width visually narrow. A very wide, deep back exposes more of the shoulder blade and can make the shoulders look broader.

## What suits narrow or sloping shoulders?

Horizontal lines add width. A [boat neck](/designs/boat-neck/) is the classic choice, and a square neck works well with silk borders. A puff or frill sleeve adds a little volume at the shoulder and balances wider hips.

Sloping shoulders need a fitting check. The shoulder seam often has to be adjusted so the sleeve does not drop down the arm, and some women need a slightly narrower neck width so the blouse does not slide off. Mention this at your trial.

## What suits a short or long neck?

For a **short neck**, deeper fronts open up the area below the chin. A deep U or V works well, as does a sweetheart neck. Avoid closed collars and high necks, and choose necklaces that sit lower.

For a **long neck**, closed and high styles look elegant, especially with simple jewellery or long earrings. A high neck with a cut-out back is a popular reception choice. Very deep, wide necks can make a long neck look even longer, which is fine if that is what you want.

## What suits a fuller bust?

V and sweetheart necks lengthen the upper body and avoid the "shelf" effect of a high neck. A princess cut usually gives smoother support than a regular darted blouse; see [princess cut vs katori blouse](/blog/princess-cut-vs-katori-blouse/). Place heavy embroidery on the sleeves, back and border rather than across the bust, and make sure the under-bust fits snugly so the blouse does not ride up.

## What suits petite frames?

Keep proportions small. A modest round or U neck, short or elbow sleeves, and fine detail rather than wide borders. Very wide necks and big sleeves can overwhelm a small frame. A slightly shorter blouse length often looks better on a petite frame because it shows more of the saree pleats.

## What suits fuller or slim arms?

For **fuller arms**, sleeve length matters more than the neck. Elbow and three-quarter sleeves give coverage, and a [sheer net sleeve](/designs/sheer-net-sleeve/) gives coverage without heaviness, which is useful in warm weather. Most importantly, make sure the armhole and sleeve round are comfortable. A tight sleeve draws more attention than a slightly relaxed one, and it is uncomfortable all day.

For **slim arms**, you can enjoy volume: puff, bell and cap sleeves all work, and so does a sleeveless blouse for evening functions.

## What if you have more than one of these?

Most people do. Prioritise in this order:

1. **Comfort** — armhole and sleeve round first.
2. **The feature you notice most** in your own photos.
3. **The saree** — a heavy silk may need an elbow sleeve whatever your arms.
4. **The occasion** — office blouses need easier necks and sleeves than reception blouses.

When two notes pull in different directions, such as broad shoulders and a short neck, look for styles that solve both. A V-neck works for both in that case.

## Do measurements change which styles work?

Yes. Neck width, shoulder width and armhole measurements decide how much room there is for a style. A boat neck on a narrow shoulder measurement, for example, can slip.
