Princess cut and katori are the two shaped blouse cuts most women ask about once they move beyond a basic darted blouse. Both give a closer fit than a regular blouse, but they do it in different ways, and the difference shows in how the saree drapes over them.

## What is the difference between princess cut and katori?

The difference is where the seams go and how the bust is shaped.

- **Princess cut:** two long seams on the front run from the shoulder (or armhole) down over the bust to the waist. The bust shape is built into these panels, so there are no separate darts.
- **Katori:** the front is split horizontally just below the bust. The lower band is fitted to the under-bust, and the upper part has small cup-shaped panels (katoris) that are gathered or seamed to shape the bust.

| Feature | Princess cut | Katori |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seam lines | Vertical, shoulder or armhole to waist | Horizontal under-bust band plus cup seams |
| Look | Smooth, sleek, modern | Rounded, defined, traditional |
| Fit at waist | Very close | Close, set by the band |
| Best fabrics | Silk, brocade, raw silk, structured blends | Silk, cotton silk, cotton |
| Embroidery | Large uninterrupted panels for work | Work usually placed on band and sleeves |
| Alteration room | Some, along the long seams | Limited once cups are cut |
| Trial needed | Yes, recommended | Yes, recommended |

## Who does a princess cut blouse suit?

Princess cut suits most body types because the seams can be shaped exactly to you. It works especially well if:

- You want a smooth fit without visible darts.
- You have a fuller bust and want support without a boxy look.
- The blouse is for a wedding or reception, where clean lines show in photos.
- You plan embroidery on the front, since the long panels give a clean canvas.

It is less forgiving if your weight is changing before an event, because a precisely shaped seam shows small changes.

## Who does a katori blouse suit?

Katori is a classic in South India for silk sarees, and many women prefer its shape because it is familiar. It suits you if:

- You like a defined, rounded bust line.
- You wear traditional silk sarees with a [round neck](/designs/round-neck/) or [paan neck](/designs/paan-neck/).
- You want a snug under-bust band that keeps the blouse from riding up.
- You are petite and want shaping without long vertical seams.

The key risk is cup placement. If the katori sits too high or too low, it looks wrong no matter how good the stitching is.

## Which is more comfortable to wear all day?

Comfort depends more on the armhole and fabric than the cut. That said:

- Princess cut spreads the fit across long seams, so it often feels even.
- Katori's under-bust band can feel firm by evening if it is cut very close.
- Both should let you raise your arms without the blouse riding up. If it does, the armhole needs adjusting, not the cut.

## Which necks and sleeves go with each cut?

**Princess cut** pairs well with a [sweetheart neck](/designs/sweetheart-neck/), V-neck and deeper backs like a deep U back, because the seams hold the blouse close to the body and stop the back from gaping.

**Katori** looks best with traditional necks and an [elbow sleeve](/designs/elbow-sleeve/) finished in the saree border.

## What are the common fitting problems with each cut?

Both cuts depend on accurate bust measurements. These are the issues we correct most often at trials:

- **Princess cut seam off the bust point:** the seam runs beside the bust instead of over it, causing a diagonal pull. Fixed by moving the seam line.
- **Princess cut too tight at the waist:** looks sleek standing but pulls when you sit.
- **Katori cups too high or low:** a fold of fabric appears above or below the bust.
- **Katori band riding up:** usually the band is too loose or the armhole too low.
- **Both:** gaping at the front neck when the neck is deep and the blouse is not padded.

Most of these are easy to correct at the first trial and difficult after embroidery or final finishing.

## What should you tell your tailor?

1. Which cut you want, and a photo of the look.
2. Whether you will wear padding or a particular bra.
3. Your bust point and the distance between bust points, ideally measured in person.
4. Whether your weight may change before the event.
5. Bring a well-fitting blouse, even if it is a different cut.
