The lining is the layer that actually touches your skin, so it often decides how comfortable a blouse or kurti feels after four hours at a function. A good lining also helps the garment hold its shape, protects embroidery threads from scratching you, and stops sheer fabric from showing too much. This guide explains which lining to choose and how to prepare it.

## What does a lining do?

- **Comfort.** It covers the back of embroidery, zari and seams, which can scratch.
- **Shape.** It gives soft fabrics enough body to hold a neckline, princess seams or padding.
- **Opacity.** It stops sheer fabrics like net, chiffon and thin georgette from being see-through.
- **Sweat protection.** It absorbs sweat before it reaches silk or zari, which helps the outer fabric last longer.
- **Easy dressing.** A smooth lining helps a fitted blouse or sleeve slide on.

## How do cotton, satin and crepe linings compare?

| Lining | Feel | Breathability | Best under | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cotton (soft voile, cambric, mul) | Soft, matte | Excellent | Silk, cotton, embroidered and bridal blouses, kurtis | Shrinks unless pre-washed |
| Satin (usually polyester) | Smooth, slippery | Poor | Velvet, net, evening wear, fitted sleeves | Heat and sweat; can slip at seams |
| Crepe | Soft with a slight grain | Moderate | Georgette, chiffon, flowing kurtis and anarkalis | Can stretch if cut off-grain |
| Silk or art silk lining | Smooth, light | Moderate | Bridal or high-end blouses for a luxurious finish | Costlier; needs dry cleaning |

For most of our customers in Coimbatore, soft cotton is the lining we recommend first. It is the one that keeps a silk blouse comfortable through a long muhurtham.

## When should you line a blouse or kurti?

Line it if any of these apply:

- The fabric is sheer: net, chiffon, thin georgette, organza.
- There is embroidery, zari, aari or maggam work on it.
- The blouse is padded or has a structured cut such as a princess or katori cut.
- The outer fabric scratches or feels rough.
- It is a light-coloured kurti that might show undergarments.

You can usually skip lining for thick handloom or khadi cotton blouses and simple cotton kurtis meant for daily wear. Leaving it out keeps them cooler and easier to wash.

## Why must cotton lining be pre-washed?

Cotton shrinks the first time it gets wet. If the lining shrinks and the outer silk does not, the blouse pulls tight, puckers along seams, or the lining starts peeking out at the neckline and sleeve edges.

How to pre-wash lining at home:

1. Soak the lining in plain cold water for about 30 minutes.
2. Squeeze gently; do not wring hard.
3. Dry in shade, flat or on a line.
4. Iron it lightly before bringing it to the tailor.

Pre-wash the outer cotton fabric too, if you plan to wash the blouse at home. Silk and embroidered pieces should not be soaked; ask us instead and we will check them before cutting.

## Which lining suits which outer fabric?

- **Pure silk or pattu:** soft cotton, for comfort and sweat absorption.
- **Cotton saree blouse:** thin cotton or no lining at all.
- **Georgette or chiffon:** crepe or soft cotton.
- **Net blouse or net lehenga choli:** cotton for daytime, satin or silk for evening; skin-tone if you want an illusion neckline such as a [sheer net yoke](/designs/sheer-net-yoke/).
- **Velvet:** satin helps it slide on, but cotton is cooler if the event is in the day.
- **Anarkali or flared kurti:** crepe or cotton that matches the flare; we discuss this in the [anarkali stitching guide](/blog/anarkali-stitching-guide/).

## Can you change the lining of an old blouse?

Yes, in many cases. If a lining has shrunk, torn or yellowed while the outer fabric is still good, it can be replaced. This involves opening the seams, so it is easier on simple blouses than on heavily embroidered ones. Bring it in and we will tell you honestly whether relining is worth it.

## Common lining problems we fix

- **Lining showing at the neckline:** usually caused by shrinkage or a lining cut slightly bigger than the outer fabric.
- **Blouse twisting on the body:** the lining was cut off-grain.
- **Itchy armholes:** zari or embroidery not fully covered at the seams.
- **Blouse feeling hot:** a polyester lining used for daytime wear.
