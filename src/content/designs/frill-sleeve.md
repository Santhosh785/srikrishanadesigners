## What is a frill sleeve?

A frill sleeve has a ruffle, gathered or pleated, stitched to its hem. The base sleeve is usually short or elbow length and fitted, and the frill adds 2 to 4 inches of soft movement below it. Some designs use two or three tiers of frills, or replace the sleeve entirely with a single layer of ruffle.

## Who it suits

Frills add volume and draw attention to the arm, so they suit slim arms, narrow shoulders and petite frames. They have a light, youthful mood that works for college students and young women as well as anyone who enjoys a playful look. On fuller arms, a single, narrow frill at the elbow is more balanced than layers.

## Best sarees and occasions

Frill sleeves look lovely with plain or printed georgette and chiffon sarees, organza sarees for parties, and cotton sarees for casual summer functions. They are also popular on half-saree blouses for young girls. With heavy Kanchipuram silk they can look busy, so a simple sleeve usually works better there.

## Tailoring notes

We cut the frill on the cross grain or as a circle, depending on how full you want it, and roll-hem the edge with a fine stitch so it stays light. The gathers are spread evenly and stitched between the sleeve and its lining so no raw edge shows inside. For crisp frills in cotton or organza we press each tier carefully; for soft georgette we let them fall naturally. If the frill is in a different fabric, we test how it drapes against your blouse before cutting. Tiered frills take more fabric, so bring a little extra.
