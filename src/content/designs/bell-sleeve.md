## What is a bell sleeve?

A bell sleeve fits the upper arm like a normal sleeve and then widens toward the hem, so the bottom flares out like a bell. The flare can start at the elbow, giving a short fitted part and a wide frill below, or the whole sleeve can be cut on a gradual A-line. Lengths vary from just above the elbow to three quarter.

## Who it suits

Because the sleeve moves away from the forearm, it hides fuller arms and draws the eye to the hand, which also balances broad shoulders. It suits most body types and ages, and is a good way to make a plain blouse look more designed without going deep at the neck.

## Best sarees and occasions

Bell sleeves are a party and festive style. They work beautifully with georgette and chiffon sarees, where the flare floats, and with soft silks and lehenga blouses for sangeet and reception evenings. They are less practical for daily or office wear.

## Tailoring notes

We draft the sleeve with a fitted cap so the shoulder stays neat, then add the flare either by slashing and spreading the pattern or by attaching a circular piece at the elbow. The hem of a bell is on the bias in places, so we let the fabric hang overnight before levelling it, otherwise it drops unevenly. In georgette the hem is finished with a narrow rolled edge; in silk it is faced or lined. A lace or border trim along the hem adds weight and helps it hang. Long bells need extra fabric, so check your blouse piece first.
