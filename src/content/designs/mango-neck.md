## What is a mango neck blouse?

The mango, or keri, is one of the oldest motifs in Indian textiles, and the mango neck turns it into a neckline. The sides drop almost straight from the shoulders, then each curves inward like the rounded body of a mango, and the two tips meet at a point in the centre. It is cut on the front or on the back, and is sometimes called a mango-shape or paisley neck.

## Who it suits

Because the neck is open at the top and narrows to a point, it lengthens the neck and slims broad shoulders without going very deep. It suits most women with a medium or short neck. On a very long neck, a high or boat neck may balance better.

## Best sarees and occasions

Mango necks echo the paisley motifs woven into many Kanchipuram and Banarasi borders, so they pair naturally with silk sarees for weddings and festivals. They also look lovely on half-saree and pavadai blouses for girls, and on lehenga blouses where the point can carry a small hanging latkan.

## Tailoring notes

The inward curves are the tricky part. We draft one side on paper, mirror it, and stay-stitch immediately after cutting so the bias curves do not stretch. The lining is attached by machine, then the curves are clipped closely and understitched so they roll in neatly. The centre point is reinforced from inside. This needs a fabric with some body, such as silk or cotton silk; soft georgette is better suited to a simple U or V. Typical depth is 6 to 7 inches. Front hooks begin below the point, or for an embroidered front we move the opening to the back.
