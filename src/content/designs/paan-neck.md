## What is a paan neck blouse?

Paan is the Tamil and Hindi word for the betel leaf, and a paan neck copies its outline. From the shoulders the neckline swells outward in two rounded curves and then narrows to a gentle point at the centre front or back. It sits somewhere between a U neck and a V neck, with more curve than either. The shape is a long-standing favourite in South Indian silk blouses.

## Who it suits

The outward curves open up the chest while the point adds a vertical line, so a paan neck flatters a short neck, a fuller bust and broader shoulders. It also frames a traditional necklace or a mango mala beautifully. On a very petite frame keep it modest in width so the curves do not reach the shoulders.

## Best sarees and occasions

Paan necks are most at home on Kanchipuram, Arani and soft silk sarees for weddings, seemantham, Deepavali and temple functions. They also suit half-saree blouses for puberty ceremonies and festive occasions. The shape is a classic place for a border of aari or zari work, or for a contrast piping in a colour from the saree border.

## Tailoring notes

The two curves must match exactly, so we draft half the neckline, fold the paper and cut both sides from the same line. The curved edges are stay-stitched and the point is reinforced before the lining is attached and understitched. A typical depth is 6 to 7 inches in front; the widest part of the curve is set by your shoulder width so it does not land on bra straps. Front hooks start just below the point. If you want piping or a narrow border, it is stitched on before lining so the curve stays smooth.
